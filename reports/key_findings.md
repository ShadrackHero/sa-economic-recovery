# Key Findings: Is South Africa's Economy Really Recovering?

**Research period:** 2010–2026  
**Data source:** Statistics South Africa  
**Research approach:** Quantitative, descriptive, and comparative analysis

## Executive Summary

South Africa's economic recovery presents a mixed picture.

Although real GDP has recovered above its pre-pandemic level, this improvement has not translated into equally strong gains in output per person, employment access, or average real earnings.

Our findings suggest that **South Africa has experienced an uneven economic recovery: aggregate economic output has recovered, but important measures of economic well-being remain weak.**

## Finding 1: Aggregate GDP Has Recovered, but Growth Remains Modest

By the second quarter of 2026, real GDP was approximately **2.24% above its level in the fourth quarter of 2019**.

However, average quarterly GDP growth declined from approximately 0.421% in the pre-pandemic comparison period to 0.252% in the post-pandemic comparison period.

The estimated difference was not statistically significant under HAC-robust inference (p = 0.210).

Historical trend extrapolations suggest that GDP remains approximately 4.34%–9.09% below selected pre-pandemic growth trajectories. These are descriptive benchmarks, not estimates of output lost specifically because of COVID-19.

**Interpretation:** Aggregate output has recovered, but the evidence does not establish a statistically significant slowdown in average quarterly growth across the selected periods.

## Finding 2: Real GDP per Person Remains Below 2019

Despite aggregate real GDP increasing by approximately 1.59% between 2019 and 2025, the estimated population increased by approximately 7.34%.

Consequently, real GDP per capita declined from approximately R120,049 in 2019 to R113,619 in 2025, representing a **5.36% decline**.

**Interpretation:** South Africa's aggregate economic recovery has not kept pace with population growth over this period.

## Finding 3: Employment Recovery Has Not Kept Pace with Population Growth

Between 2019 Q3 and 2026 Q3:

- Employment increased by approximately 2.84%.
- The working-age population increased by approximately 9.96%.
- The official unemployment rate increased from 29.1% to 33.7%.
- The employment-to-population ratio declined from 42.4% to 39.7%.

Applying the 2019 Q3 employment-to-population ratio to the 2026 Q3 working-age population produces a descriptive employment shortfall of approximately **1.17 million people** relative to that benchmark.

This is not a causal estimate of pandemic-related job losses or the number of available vacancies.

**Interpretation:** The number of employed people has increased, but employment has not kept pace with growth in the working-age population.

## Finding 4: Consumer Prices Have Increased Substantially

Between October 2019 and October 2026, South Africa's headline CPI increased by approximately **38.55%**.

A representative basket costing R1,000 in October 2019 would therefore cost approximately R1,385.50 in October 2026, assuming its prices changed in line with headline CPI.

This represents a cumulative increase in the consumer price level, not the annual inflation rate.

**Interpretation:** Households face a substantially higher general price level than before the pandemic.

## Finding 5: Average Real Earnings Have Barely Increased

Average monthly nominal earnings in the formal non-agricultural sector increased from R22,500 in November 2019 to R30,611 in May 2026.

Over those same reference months:

- Nominal average earnings increased by 36.05%.
- Headline CPI increased by 35.58%.
- Inflation-adjusted average earnings increased by approximately **0.35%**.

Expressed in November 2019 purchasing power, average May 2026 earnings were equivalent to approximately R22,578.

These are average formal-sector earnings, not median earnings or a measure of wage growth for every individual worker.

**Interpretation:** Average nominal earnings largely kept pace with inflation, but showed almost no improvement in purchasing power.

## Overall Assessment

The evidence supports three conclusions:

1. **Aggregate recovery:** Real GDP has recovered above its late-2019 level.
2. **Incomplete per-person and labour-market recovery:** Real GDP per capita and the employment-to-population ratio remain below their respective 2019 benchmarks.
3. **Limited purchasing-power improvement:** Consumer prices have increased substantially, while average real earnings in the formal non-agricultural sector have barely increased.

Taken together, the results suggest that **economic recovery measured through national output is not equivalent to a broad-based recovery in economic well-being.**

## Research Limitations

The indicators use different reference periods, and the analysis does not establish the causal contribution of COVID-19 or individual economic policies.

GDP per capita is not household disposable income. CPI does not measure each household's personal cost of living, and average QES earnings do not represent all workers.

The findings should therefore be interpreted as a multidimensional assessment of recovery rather than a single measure of national prosperity.

## Final Conclusion

**South Africa's economy has recovered in aggregate output, but that recovery remains uneven.**

The central challenge is not simply whether GDP has returned to its pre-pandemic level, but whether economic growth is translating into stronger employment, greater output per person, and meaningful improvements in purchasing power.
