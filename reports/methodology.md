# Methodology: South Africa's Economic Recovery

## Research Question

Has South Africa's economy recovered from the COVID-19 economic shock, and to what extent has that recovery translated into improvements in living standards, employment, and purchasing power?

## Data Sources

The investigation uses official Statistics South Africa publications and datasets:

- **Real GDP:** Quarterly national accounts, publication P0441.
- **Population:** Mid-year population estimates, publication P0302.
- **Labour market:** Quarterly Labour Force Survey (QLFS).
- **Consumer prices:** Consumer Price Index (CPI), historical monthly series.
- **Earnings:** Quarterly Employment Statistics (QES), publication P0277, December 2019 and June 2026.

## Analytical Methods

### 1. Aggregate Economic Recovery

Real GDP in 2026 Q2 is compared with 2019 Q4 to assess whether aggregate economic output has returned to its pre-pandemic level.

Pre-pandemic and post-pandemic quarterly growth rates are also compared using heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent (HAC) inference.

Historical trend extrapolations provide descriptive benchmarks rather than causal estimates of economic losses.

### 2. Real GDP per Capita

Annual real GDP is divided by estimated population to measure inflation-adjusted economic output per person.

The analysis compares 2019 and 2025. Population estimates are reconstructed using published annual population growth rates, introducing approximation uncertainty.

### 3. Labour-Market Recovery

QLFS indicators are compared between 2019 Q3 and 2026 Q3, including employment, unemployment, the unemployment rate, and the employment-to-population ratio.

A descriptive employment benchmark estimates the number of people who would have been employed in 2026 Q3 if the 2019 Q3 employment-to-population ratio had prevailed.

This benchmark does not represent a causal estimate of jobs lost or unfilled vacancies.

### 4. Consumer Price Changes

Headline CPI is used to calculate cumulative consumer price growth between October 2019 and October 2026.

Cumulative price changes measure changes in the price level, not changes in the annual inflation rate.

### 5. Real Earnings and Purchasing Power

Average monthly nominal earnings from QES are compared between November 2019 and May 2026.

The CPI series is used to express May 2026 earnings in November 2019 purchasing power.

Real earnings growth is calculated as:

\[
\text{Real Growth} =
\left(
\frac{\text{Nominal Earnings}_{2026}/\text{Nominal Earnings}_{2019}}
{\text{CPI}_{2026}/\text{CPI}_{2019}}
-1
\right)\times100
\]

QES average earnings cover formal non-agricultural employees and may reflect changes in workforce composition. They do not measure median earnings or the experience of every worker.

## Interpretation and Limitations

- The five indicators use different observation periods and cannot be combined into a single recovery percentage.
- GDP growth does not necessarily imply improvements in household welfare or income distribution.
- GDP per capita is an aggregate average, not a direct measure of household income.
- Employment comparisons are descriptive and do not establish causal effects of the pandemic.
- CPI measures average consumer price changes; individual households may experience different inflation rates.
- QES average earnings do not represent all employed South Africans.
- The study does not identify causal effects of specific government policies.

## Central Finding

South Africa's aggregate real GDP has recovered above its late-2019 level, but recovery is substantially weaker when assessed through real GDP per capita, employment access, and average real earnings.

The evidence supports a conclusion of **uneven economic recovery**, rather than either complete recovery or the absence of recovery.
